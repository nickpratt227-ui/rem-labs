Smart scale vs DEXA scan: what each measures

Both promise a body-composition number, but they work very differently.

How a smart scale works

Body-composition scales use bioelectrical impedance (BIA). A small, safe current passes between the electrodes, and the scale estimates fat and lean mass from the resistance plus your height, age and sex. Four-electrode scales read through the feet. Eight-electrode scales add hand grips, so they can estimate arms, legs and trunk separately.

BIA readings shift with hydration, meals, exercise and time of day. Weigh at the same time, under the same conditions, and watch the trend rather than any one reading.

How a DEXA scan works

DEXA (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry) passes two low-dose X-ray beams over your body to measure bone, fat and lean tissue by region. It's done in a clinic by trained staff, costs money per scan, and uses a small radiation dose.

Which to use

Smart scaleDEXA
WhereHome, dailyClinic, occasionally
MeasuresEstimates from impedanceImaging-based measurement
Best forDay-to-day trends and habitsA detailed baseline or check-in

Many people combine them: an occasional scan for a baseline, and a home scale for the daily habit. A smart scale is not a substitute for a clinical assessment.
